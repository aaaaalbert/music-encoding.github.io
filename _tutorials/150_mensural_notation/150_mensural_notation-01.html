Welcome to the MEI Mensural Notation tutorial!

This tutorial teaches you the basics of transcribing music that has been written or printed in mensural notation into MEI, an XML-based music markup format.

The goal we are following in this tutorial is a transcription that is as semantically correct as possible – i.e., marks up the notes, staves, lyrics etc. using appropriate elements of MEI –, while accepting certain inadequacies of the current state of technology when it comes to visual representation, e.g. the exact shapes of clefs.

Preliminaries

We assume that you are familiar with MEI already and know how to encode CMN (Common Music Notation, also called Common Western Music Notation). For example, you should know how <staff> and <note> work. Refreshers are available in a separate tutorial and the MEI guidelines.

We assume further that you know mensural notation already. We will focus on white mensural notation in this tutorial, but black mensural and trecento notations can be encoded as well; separate MEI modules exist for neumes and tablatures.

Keeping up to date

Finally, please note that MEI is under constant development. This tutorial tries to keep up, but it can happen that it gets out of sync. Apologies for that, and please let us know if you find errors in the tutorial! For “ground truth” about the MEI standard, always refer to the MEI guidelines and the MEI Schema.
